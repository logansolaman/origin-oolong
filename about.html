A buyer in Hong Kong, a farm in Zhangping.

Origin Oolong LLC is an independent trading company. We buy single lots from one village in Fujian, document them, and sell them to people who have to be able to reorder.

The place

Zhangping sits inland in Fujian, in Longyan prefecture, around 113 kilometres from Xiamen. It is not on the standard oolong map that begins with Wuyi and ends with the Taiwanese high mountains. That omission is the opportunity: no incumbent owns the name in English, and the tea is good enough to be compared with both.

Shuixian is the cultivar. It is a large leaf variety, higher oxidation than a green style tieguanyin, lower than a Wuyi rock tea, and it takes charcoal without losing its floral top. Yongfu, the town we buy from, is a valley of small holdings rather than one estate, which is why lot level documentation matters more here than a farm certificate.

The same village sells into the domestic market at prices that embarrass the export price. That gap is not a secret and it is not ours to keep. It is closed by work: picking the right lots, testing them, documenting them, and being able to repeat them.

The master

Lots are made under Lin Zhi-Chun, the farm's tea master. He holds the Chinese craft titles for tea making and is recognised as an inheritor of the regional making tradition. Those titles are only worth citing because they come with a name, a face and a method: the shake and rest schedule in 做青 is decided by hand each day, on that day's leaf and weather, and cannot be written into a machine setting.

What we are not

Not the farm. Not the farm's consumer brand, which sells direct under its own name in its own markets. Not a shop, and not a catalogue of ninety origins. We are the buyer who takes title, cups the lot before it ships, and answers for it afterwards.

Why the range is short.

Oolong is the hardest tea class to buy, because the process is decided by hand, daily, and no chemical standard describes the result. That makes variance structural rather than accidental.

How we trade

We take title. That means we buy the lot, hold the risk, and sell it on. It also means we can tell a buyer no, because we are not earning a commission on a transaction we do not control.

Terms are on the website rather than in the third email: FOB Xiamen, fifty percent deposit, Hong Kong arbitration, a fourteen day claim window against the retained sample. Volume commitments earn written market exclusivity, which is the thing specialist buyers ask for and rarely get.
