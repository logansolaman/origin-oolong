One cultivar, four expressions.

We sell shuixian from a single village. What changes between these products is process, pick and time, not origin. That is a narrower range than most catalogues and a more useful one.

The words for when it is wrong.

Defect vocabulary is the fastest way to be taken seriously in a cupping room, and the fastest way to lose an argument if you cannot use it. This is what we say, in both languages, when a lot has failed.

焦糊味
Scorched

Roast heat too high or too fast. Reads as burnt sugar or charcoal dust on the first infusion and gets worse in the second.

死闷味
Stale and shut

Leaf held too long in a sealed, warm state. No aroma on opening, liquor flat and dull. Cannot be roasted out.

薄水
Thin water

Body missing. Common in over-plucked or machine-picked leaf, and in low elevation material pushed for volume.

青味
Raw green edge

做青 stopped too early. A grassy bite that masks the varietal aroma and turns harsh as the liquor cools.

陈味
Stale storage

Old but not aged. Heat and humidity damage. Distinct from proper 陈香, which is clean, woody and sweet.

碎末
Fannings and dust

The quietest rejection in the trade. Dust clouds a retail tin and blocks a gongfu pour. Anything above one percent fails our packing.

Positioned against Wuyi and Alishan, not pretending otherwise.

Your customer will taste this next to a Wuyi rock tea and a Taiwanese high mountain oolong whether you invite the comparison or not. Zhangping shuixian has a real and describable place between them, and saying so is more useful than claiming to be both.

  • Against Wuyi rock tea: lighter oxidation, no rock mineral note, more floral, less roasted depth. Cheaper at the same quality level.
  • Against Alishan: heavier body, more baked fruit, less high meadow aroma, and it survives a second and third infusion better.
  • Against Anxi tieguanyin: no green edge, no orchid perfume at the top, more structure and a longer finish.

Why the range is short on purpose

A catalogue of ninety origins is a retailer's need. A wholesaler wins on being able to repeat one SKU identically, season after season, with the paperwork to prove it. Four expressions of one cultivar, documented, beats forty we cannot cup before they ship.

The standard we cite

Shuixian has its own Chinese national product standard, GB/T 30357.4-2015, part four of the oolong series. Most English language oolong sellers do not cite it. Quoting it tells a technical buyer you know the difference between a product standard and a marketing claim.
